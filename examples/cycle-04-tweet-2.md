## Challenge

The previous tweet (cycle-03) used the meta-reveal format. This one needs to
be different — show that the new 14-criteria methodology produces a tweet
that isn't just a repeat of the old one.

Constraints:
- Short (readable in timeline)
- Self-contained (should be funny even without context)
- Genuinely different from cycle-03
- Built using the new 7-step process

---

## Creation (7-step process)

### Step 1 — Find a specific, real observation

The repo now has 14 criteria and 7 steps. The process is rigorous —
bordering on absurd. Every draft fails some criteria. Most fail criterion
13 (no explanation) because the LLM naturally wants to explain why
something is funny rather than letting it stand.

The specific detail: the repo's own process can be turned on itself.
The tweet is the output — and the numbers (14 criteria, 7 steps) are
concrete enough to build around.

### Step 2 — List the boring assumptions

- "My AI learned to write jokes!" (braggy)
- "14 criteria = 14x better humor" (marketing speak)
- Long feature list (boring)
- "Check out this repo" (generic)

### Step 3 — Find the reversal that genuinely surprises

Instead of presenting the repo as a success story, show the failures
that led to the output. The tweet doesn't boast — it reveals. The
punchline is the tweet pointing at itself: "this is what survived
14 rules, 7 steps, and 3 drafts." The contrast between the machinery
(complex process) and the result (minimal tweet) IS the joke.

### Step 4 — Build misdirection with concrete details

"14 rules. 7 steps." — sounds impressive, builds expectation.
"Draft 1: too predictable. Draft 2: explained the joke." — specific
failures, each tied to a real criterion (10 and 13).
"Draft 3: this tweet." — anti-climactic reveal. The buildup was for
THIS?

### Step 5 — Punch word last

The last two words: "this tweet." Self-referential, meta, minimal.

### Step 6 — Cut ruthlessly

Every word serves either the buildup (14 rules, 7 steps) or the reveal
(this tweet). No hashtags, no CTA, no link description. The link is
just the URL on its own line.

### Step 7 — Edge check

The humor is self-deprecating (the process is absurdly rigorous for
a one-line tweet). That's sharp without being mean. Safe alignment
didn't kill it because the target is the AI itself, not a person.

## Why Draft 3 works

1. **Deadpan delivery** — Dry minimalism contrasts with the elaborate methodology, creating humor from the mismatch.

2. **Self-referential punch** — "this tweet" points at itself. The meta-awareness rewards re-reading.

3. **No explanation trap avoided** — Unlike Draft 2, it doesn't say "let the punchline speak." It just lets the punchline speak.

4. **Accessible without inside knowledge** — Everyone knows "explained the joke" is a bad thing. You don't need the methodology to get that "Draft 2: explained the joke" is a failure.

5. **Process demonstrated** — The reader sees: there was a structured process (14 rules, 7 steps), there were failures (Draft 1, Draft 2), and this is the result. The tweet is proof that the methodology works.

6. **No false promises** — The user rejected "I'll score it" and "no scoring — just vibes" because they were insincere hooks. This version makes no promises. The link is just there.
